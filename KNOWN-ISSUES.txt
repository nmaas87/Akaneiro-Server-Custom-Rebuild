KNOWN ISSUES AND LIMITATIONS
============================

The server is complete enough to play the whole game, but a few things are different from the
original. They fall into three groups: behaviour of the game client that a server cannot change,
features of the original online service that are not built, and rules the original server had
that are lost and were rebuilt by assumption.


CLIENT BEHAVIOUR (CANNOT BE FIXED ON THE SERVER)
------------------------------------------------

The game client is used as it is, so these stay:

- Missions on "Low" end after their first task. That is the original mission data: on Low every
  mission has a single task, the full story with its boss starts on Medium. (Recordings of the
  original game show the same.) The stone gate to the boss in A2_M2, for example, only opens in a
  Medium task.
- "Task complete: Unkonw Task" (sic) appears after some tasks. The server sends the correct branch
  and task ids; the client looks the name up in its own mission data and does not find it there.
  Its data also calls every step of "Raging Ushi Oni" "Wicked Beasts".
- Quick slots only point to the bag slot: an item put on the quick bar still occupies its bag
  slot.
- Mission timer, minimap, the shimmer on switches and the hand/kunai mouse cursor are drawn by the
  client and behave as it decides. The minimap's radar shows monsters and yourself only: companion
  allies and village NPCs never get a blip, and the map pictures and radar areas are fixed in the
  client.
- Crafting materials show a value of 0 in their tooltip: the client only computes values for
  equipment. Selling them still pays (a fifth of their shop price each).
- The "speed up" panel of the mission cooldown does not send anything to the server. That is why
  mission cooldowns are switched off (see below).
- Thrown balls start at the monster's centre, not in its hand (the client's projectile code).
- The crystal shop opens in the Steam overlay when the client wants crystals, for example when
  karma is short for a training. That page does not exist anymore, so the overlay shows an error
  page; close it. Crystal prices are free on this server, karma prices are not.
- The client asks for the "friend reward" twice every time a map loads. The server answers only
  once per visit, otherwise the mission summary screen would close right after opening.
- Reconnecting to an account that is already playing: the client shows its "X is still online,
  reconnect?" panel and the shipped builds answer "No" on their own. That is exactly what the
  server needs: the old (usually already dead) connection is closed, and the new login loads the
  account fresh from disk, so the zombie's saves can never roll back progress. A connection that
  died without saying goodbye is dropped by the server itself after 45 seconds without the
  client's 10-second ping.
- The elder's shop (Elder Shaman Pan) only stocks Food and Drink; its "Bundles" and "Materials"
  tabs are empty. The shipped data table (ConsumableItem.General) has ten food rows, ten potion
  rows and no bundle or material rows at all, and the message the original service used to push
  shop offers is a log-only stub in this client build - a server cannot restock those tabs.
  Nothing is lost: crafting materials come from monster drops. (Checked live on the Steam
  client: the screen and its two full tabs open, and buying potions and food works.)
- Goto, the rare vendor in the village (right of Cato), answers to nothing when clicked. The
  shipped client's "rare vendor" click branch is an empty stub - it sends no request and opens
  no screen (confirmed in the client's own ShopNpc code and in its runtime log). Nothing is
  lost: Cato's shop already asks the server for both offer lists, and Goto's four rare items
  are in Cato's list (see the price note under ASSUMPTIONS). The other village NPCs - Cato,
  Craftsman Resak (crafting), the elder, the trainers, the well, the stash, the bounty board -
  all open normally in the shipped client.


NOT BUILT: THE ORIGINAL ONLINE FEATURES
---------------------------------------

Akaneiro had no real-time co-op: the network protocol has no messages for other players in the
same level. Its online side was social and asynchronous, and most of it does not exist on this
server (the summon window's friend ally is covered under ASSUMPTIONS):

- Chat with other players. Messages are only echoed back to the sender. Since several players can
  be connected to one server, this one could be added.
- Leaderboards, events, gift and redeem codes. Codes are never valid, event reports are accepted
  and ignored. The mailbox holds the system letters every new character gets (starter pack and
  Kickstarter rewards); claiming their attachments works, and "Accept all" into a nearly full
  bag takes what still fits and leaves the rest on the letter instead of destroying it.
- Buying crystals with real money. Not possible; instead, everything that costs crystals is free
  (revivals, stash tabs, crystal items, spirit rentals priced in crystals, finishing trainings
  early, crafting with crystals).
- Well upgrades were bought with crystals and are not offered by this client: the well stays on
  level 1 (100 karma every 15 minutes).
- Steam login checks. The server does not verify Steam tickets or DLC ownership; every login is
  accepted. Fine on your own PC or LAN, unsafe on the open internet (see HOSTING.txt).
- The web login of the old "client ver" build (email + password at client.php) is implemented
  (HOSTING.txt, "Web login"); its account service is this server, tokens live only in memory and
  there is no password-recovery mail. The browser-based WebPlayer variant (WebLoginCtrl, a GET
  with ip:port answer) is not implemented: it needs the actual WebPlayer build plus a host page.
- Pre-2014 client builds get packets in their own era's layout through server-side dialect
  layers, chosen by the "other" version field of the web login: the 2013 Chinese build omits the
  field (Zh2013Dialect), the April 2013 English download client posts its build version
  20130416 (Apr2013Dialect), 2014 clients post a 2014-era version and get the plain wire.
  The January 2013 build knows no mailbox, no stash and no well, the April 2013 build only no
  mailbox/stash/slots - welcome letters (including the rare catalog letter) are therefore only
  visible from a 2014 client, and claiming them must happen there.
- Content is per client era only as far as the era's files exist. April 2013 sessions play on
  that client's own item/level/reward/monster tables (client-source-2013 plus
  GameData2013/monsters.json) and their mission and area ids are translated between the 2013
  and 2014 families. The map LAYOUTS cannot be era-split: the 2013 client ships no spawner,
  trigger or breakable placement at all (that lived on the lost original server - verified
  empty across all 140+ bundles), so all eras share the extracted 2014 placement; the maps
  themselves are the same geometry (2013 spawn coordinates match to the metre). April sessions
  do only know the 2013 monster set (92 of the 134 types): a 2014-only monster a spawner
  references simply never appears there, which is what the April client could draw anyway.
  The 2013 Chinese sessions stay on the modern tables but are gated and priced to their build:
  only the fifteen maps it bundles (Acts 1-5, three per region) ever light up on its bounty
  map, its region unlocks and in-place revivals charge the price ladders of the tables bundled
  inside that client, its tutorial runs the era's 29-wolf waves, and its daily gifts land in
  the bag without a gift screen (that cut's reward handlers are empty stubs).
  Characters mixed across eras also keep their old ids: an item created under 2014 content can
  be a row the 2013 client cannot draw (pictureless) until it is replaced.

Several players can use one server at the same time, but each plays in their own world and they
never meet.


APPROXIMATIONS
--------------

- Monster pathfinding. The level files contain no navigation mesh (the client computes it from
  the level geometry when it loads). The server learns where the ground is walkable from the
  player's own trail, spawn points and wander routes, and monsters follow those ways around
  obstacles. In an area the player has not walked yet, a monster may take a straighter line than
  the client would.
- Abilities only damage monsters. Baskets, chests and switches break from normal attacks.
- A spirit helper that levels up during a mission shows its new level the next time the spirit
  list is opened.


ASSUMPTIONS (ORIGINAL FORMULAS LOST)
------------------------------------

The original server code is lost. Where the client's data does not say how something works, the
server uses these rules. They are the first place to look when something feels off balance.

- Revival with an item (the Shinigami Scroll option) spends one scroll from the active
  character's bag; the counter on the account screen counts the scrolls in every bag of the
  account. Without a scroll the revive is refused, like on the original. The 2013 builds have
  no scroll items: April's death menu pays an account charge instead (or crystals when the
  counter is empty), and the January build only offers its shard revival at all. Each era is
  charged the ladder its own client shows: April uses the 2014 shard ladder (assumption), the
  January build its bundled RevivalCost.Costs table (fact - the panel displays it).
- Summoning a companion as an ally works, with stand-ins: the original took real other-player
  characters from the friend network, and that network is long gone. The summon button offers
  three invented companions - one per discipline (Kenshin, Benkei and Koga) - at your character's
  level, wearing the discipline's weapon and clothing at an item level that fits, with the
  abilities the game gives every ally; they fight about as well as a hunter of your level. Hiring
  is free and lasts 30 minutes (the values the window itself shows), one ally at a time; the
  companion comes along into the next map, heals when it stands out of the fight, and a fallen
  one frees the slot. The client walks it and decides what it does, the server judges every hit,
  and a monster goes for whichever of the two stands closer. Its kills pay you full experience
  and loot. The friend hire reward (what the original gave you when friends hired your character)
  stays empty - there is nobody to hire you.
  Caveat - client generation: no shipped client build actually runs this feature. Both client
  builds of this era (the 2014-04-16 web build and the Steam build, which is the same
  proto 20140110 app) compile the whole ally chain into empty stubs: the HUD's "add ally" click
  handler and the friend-ally packet handlers (FriendAllyEnter, GetFriendListResult,
  SelectFriendAllyResult) are empty functions, the HUD never registers the ally-info-group
  template ("+" button), and the old EZGUI summon window is attached to no prefab or scene in
  any build or in the public-domain project. Nothing the server sends changes that - on the
  stock clients there is no companion UI at all. A modified client (IL patch of the shipped
  Assembly-CSharp.dll, or the public-domain client project with the flow implemented) lets the
  complete server side drive companions normally.
- Act 8 stays closed. The mission tables still carry the three cut maps of Act 8 ("Knock, Knock",
  "Fire in the Dark", "What Lies Below"), and the chain even unlocks them after beating Act 7's
  "Wicked Beasts" - but the shipped client has no level, no scene XML and no minimap picture for
  them: choosing one hangs the loading screen and leaves the minimap on the last map's picture.
  The live game evidently never opened these maps either, so the server does not unlock them.
- The revive slam: whenever a revive succeeds the client fires its shockwave ability, and the
  server answers as the tables say - damage to everything within 5 m (from the ability row),
  the struck monsters stagger for the 400 ms the knockback status prescribes and are shoved
  away (how hard is the one number the tables do not carry), and the risen player takes no
  damage for the 7 s ("Revival Invulnerability") or 12 s (scroll/crystal revive) the status
  table gives. Item and crystal revivals use the stronger RevivePrayer variant of the slam.
- Spirit levels belong to the kind, not to one rental: the rental keeps the shop id it was
  bought under (the client only has one 3D model per spirit for exactly that id), and the
  growth the hunter's kills earn shows up in the spirit list's level and experience fields.
  The list carries the remaining rental time, so its bar keeps counting down when the shop
  is opened again.
- Basic attacks are rate-limited on the server: a swing must land before the next one starts.
  Clients that respect their own attack animation never notice; a spamming client just gets its
  extra requests ignored.
- A mission can only be accepted when its map is unlocked for that character (the mission map's
  own rules, plus the tutorial, which every new character is in). Unknown or locked mission ids
  are answered with the failure message instead of starting something.
- Experience: monsters give 4 times the experience in their prefab.
- Kill chains: GOOD from 3 kills, LETHAL from 6, SLAYER from 10, paying 50 / 75 / 100 % of the
  chain's experience as a bonus (matches recordings: 3 wolves +18 XP, 6 wolves +54 XP). A chain
  ends 3 seconds after the last kill.
- Threat levels: beating a threat level unlocks the next one of the same map.
- Mission cooldowns are switched off: maps can be replayed at once. The original rules (the n-th
  run within a day rests for the n-th entry of MissionsThreatCycle) are implemented in MissionMap
  and can be switched on with CooldownsEnabled.
- Vendor prices: buying costs item level x base value (2.5 times what selling pays); the rare
  vendor's items are 2 levels higher and cost twice as much; 16 + 4 offers, replaced every 12
  hours. Selling equipment pays exactly the tooltip value; stackables sell for a fifth of their
  shop price.
- Armor: armor = the worn pieces (as their tooltips show) + 5 per point of DEF; it takes away
  armor / (armor + 2000) of a monster's hit (constants from the client's GameConfig.xml), at most
  75 %. On level 1 that is below 10 %.
- Talents: one point per level from level 2; the discipline picked at creation counts as one more.
  Per point: Prowess 30/10/20, Fortitude 20/30/10, Cunning 10/20/30 POW/DEF/SKILL (from the
  client).
- Starting masteries: the two the character creation screen shows for the discipline, on level 1.
- Character numbers: critical chance 5 % + 0.025 % per SKILL point, critical hits do 135 %,
  +0.2 % damage per POW point. Gems, enchantments, elements, masteries, food and spirits work as
  their descriptions say. Food without a number in its text: "small boost" +25 % (speed and crit
  +10, experience +25 %), "more than doubles" +110 %, regeneration +50 % / +100 %. Health does not
  regenerate on its own (only through potions, food, spirits and the Heavy Armor mastery); energy
  refills in about 25 seconds.
- Crafting materials: normal monsters drop one 6 % of the time, bosses 1-2, chests 35 % (55 % of
  them enchantment materials, 25 % gems, 17 % element cores, 3 % karma cores). None in the
  tutorial.
- Daily reward: follows the DayReward table; after its last day it starts over, and a missed day
  starts over at day 1.
- Looping spawners: every one in the levels has a loop count of 0, read as "do not repeat".
- Wandering monsters rest 2 seconds at each waypoint.
